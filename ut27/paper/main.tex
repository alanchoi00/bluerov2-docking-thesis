\begin{abstract}
Moored and suspended docking stations move with the waves, so the terminal visual stage of underwater docking must track a moving target. The natural extension of a static-dock pipeline is to estimate the dock velocity onboard and feed it forward into the vehicle command. We report what happened when that was done for a passive-marker docking stage on a low-cost ROV in simulation with the production autopilot in the loop: a reactive baseline docks at every sway period, the feedforward method fails at the 8 and 6~s periods of the intended site, and its own signals blame the estimator. Rebuilding every measurement from the recorded trials shows the estimate is accurate and the failure is control. The feedforward over-drives a plant whose identified gain is 2.7 against the 1.3 to 1.6 it was tuned to, and the apparent phantom velocity is dead-reckoning during the marker blackout the over-swing causes; an oracle fed the true dock velocity over-swings identically. Two repairs follow. Rescaling the open-loop gain to the identified plant docks without contact at every period from 20 to 6~s, fastest of any arm. Closing the feedforward as a body-velocity setpoint on the navigation velocity docks without contact from 20 to 8~s and needs no plant gain, at the cost of a slower approach. Under first-order Gauss-Markov navigation error every camera-based estimator absorbs a slow velocity bias one for one; the original law carried it into the command at 2.7 times its size and docked in 4 of 18 trials, the rescaled law carries it once and docks in 16 of 18, and the velocity loop cancels it and docks in 17 of 18.
\end{abstract}
