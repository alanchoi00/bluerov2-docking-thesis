\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What the oracle decided.} Arm D feeds the true dock velocity through the same law as arm B, so it isolates the plant from the estimator. It over-swings exactly as B does at every period, which places the over-swing in the open-loop law and the plant, and it fails at 6~s where nothing open-loop docks. It also seats at 8~s where B does not, in two of three trials without contact, so at that one period the estimate's phase decides the outcome on top of the over-swing: the constant-velocity filter's velocity is accurate in amplitude but lags, and the lagged feedforward through a lag-limited plant is what carries the small markers out of view. The velocity-closed arm removes the over-swing at every period with the same estimate, and is the only arm without a contact down to 8~s, so the mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:failure} and the velocity-closed feedforward of Section~\ref{sec:redesign} are both confirmed.

\textbf{Transfer to hardware.} The cliff sits where the plant lag becomes comparable to the sway period. The autopilot is the production firmware, and the rest of the lag is the vehicle's mass and added mass under bounded thrust. A real vehicle adds thruster response, the autopilot's own filtering and tether drag, so the lag can only grow and an open-loop feedforward would fail at the same or longer periods; the velocity-closed feedforward identifies the lag and places its bandwidth under it.

\textbf{Navigation error.} The observability limit of Section~\ref{sec:navobs} has a plain practical reading, and the live sweep shows it. Any onboard dock-velocity estimate carries the vehicle's own slow velocity error, whatever frame it is estimated in, so an open-loop feedforward on a real navigation solution drives the vehicle at the dock velocity plus the bias, which is what removed the original method from the dock at a 3~cm/s drift. Closing the feedforward on the same navigation solution cancels the bias, 17 of 18 trials against 4 of 18, which makes the velocity loop of Eq.~\eqref{eq:vloop} the part of the fix that matters most for a deployment, ahead of the choice of estimator. The reactive baseline is the arm that navigation drift hurts most, since a constant-position filter in a drifting frame has nothing to absorb the drift with; a docking stage that relies on it needs a navigation solution good to a centimetre per second.

\textbf{The reactive baseline.} It docks at every period, including 6~s, and it is the fastest to seat. It is also the arm with the largest entry-plane offsets from 8~s down and the one that contacts the aperture most often there: it does not track the dock, it rides the swing and latches when the phase is favourable, which a funnel dock would forgive.

\textbf{Where the fix stops.} Two limits were reached and neither is in the loop itself. At 6~s the coarse handoff gate reads the velocity loop's phase lag as relative motion and rarely latches, so the arm holds the dock to 3~cm and waits. At a 10~cm/s navigation drift the world-frame filter must carry sway plus drift above its 0.2~m/s velocity bound, the estimate the setpoint is built on lags, and one trial in six is lost at 10~s. Both are margins set for the reactive baseline on a fixed dock and kept fixed across arms.

\textbf{Limitations.} Contact is not modelled; success is scored at the capture envelope and would-be structural contacts are censored, which the simulation cannot show. The dock motion is a single prescribed sinusoid at one amplitude, a reproducible benchmark rather than a sea state; the estimators are deliberately model-free about the motion so that the conclusions do not depend on it. The nominal sweep uses ground-truth navigation, which is what made the mechanism identifiable and is also why the navigation-error sweep is reported separately. The plant gain and time constant come from open-loop step tests in the simulator and arm C's velocity loop is tuned against them, so the same tests must be repeated on hardware before the gains transfer. Before the sim-time fix of the filter node, the filter stepped on wall-clock time and its velocity state scaled with the real-time factor; the nominal sweep ran at a real-time factor of 0.99 and is unaffected, whereas the replication host ran at 0.69 and its velocity-state amplitudes are biased low.
