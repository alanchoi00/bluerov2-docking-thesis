\section{Velocity-Closed Feedforward and Estimator}
\label{sec:redesign}

Section~\ref{sec:failure} locates the failure in the path from the estimated dock velocity to the vehicle's response, and in what the estimator does while it has no measurements. Arm C therefore changes those two things, and through a change of units the effective gain of the position loop, as described below.

\subsection{Velocity-closed feedforward}

The original method adds $K_{ff}\,\hat{v}_d$ directly to an effort-like command, so the vehicle's velocity response is $K_{ff}\,g$ times the dock velocity for whatever plant gain $g$ the mode and the trim happen to produce. The velocity-closed feedforward treats the sum of the feedback command and the rotated dock velocity as a body-frame velocity setpoint and closes it on the vehicle's own odometry velocity:
\begin{align}
v_{\mathrm{ref}} &= C(e) + R_b^{w\,\top}\hat{v}_d, \nonumber\\
u &= K_p\,(v_{\mathrm{ref}} - \hat{v}_v) + K_i\!\int (v_{\mathrm{ref}} - \hat{v}_v)\,dt,
\label{eq:vloop}
\end{align}
where $C(e)$ is the position feedback on the body-frame error with the gains of arm B, $\hat{v}_v$ is the navigation velocity in the body frame, and $u$ is the effort command. The structure is the cascade of marine motion control \cite{fossen2011handbook}, an outer position loop whose output, plus the target velocity, is the reference of an inner velocity loop. The gains of $C(e)$ were kept rather than rescaled, so its output is now read as m/s per metre of error instead of effort per metre. On the identified plant this lowers the position loop's crossover from about 2.1 to 0.8~rad/s, which is part of why arm C seats more slowly (Section~\ref{sec:results}) and a confound the comparison with arm B carries. The plant gain no longer sets the vehicle's amplitude; the loop drives the measured velocity to the setpoint whatever $g$ is, and the integral term removes the steady error that a mis-scaled gain would otherwise leave. The loop gains follow from the first-order plant identified in the step tests of Section~\ref{sec:failure}: with the integral time equal to the plant time constant $T$ the open loop is $K_p g / (T s)$, a first-order closed loop whose bandwidth $K_p g / T$ is placed at 2~rad/s, below the plant's corner $1/T = 2.5$~rad/s, to keep margin against the lag a real vehicle adds ($K_p = 0.30$ effort per m/s, $K_i = 0.74$ effort per m, with conditional integration against windup). On the identified plant the loop follows an 8~s sway at 0.93 amplitude with 21$^\circ$ of lag and a 6~s sway at 0.88 with 28$^\circ$; the position feedback in the setpoint trims the remainder. In this mode the coarse controller's distance ramp on the feedforward is retained but its gain conversion is not, and the fine controller passes the dock velocity at unit gain as before. The loop has a second property that Section~\ref{sec:results} tests directly: a slowly varying error $b_v$ in the navigation velocity appears in $\hat{v}_d$ (Section~\ref{sec:navobs}) and in $\hat{v}_v$ alike, so it cancels in $v_{\mathrm{ref}} - \hat{v}_v$ and the vehicle follows the true dock velocity. An open-loop feedforward has no such cancellation: it drives the vehicle at $K_{ff}\,g$ times the bias, 2.7 times in the original fine phase, once with the gain corrected. The simplest repair consistent with Section~\ref{sec:failure} is therefore the open-loop law with $K_{ff} = 1/g$ in both phases, which removes the over-swing but still carries the bias; it is run as arm E, so that the gain error and the open-loop structure are separated in the data rather than argued.

\subsection{Bounded velocity state during blackout}

The constant-velocity filter keeps integrating its velocity state while no measurement is accepted, which produced the metre-scale excursions of Section~\ref{sec:failure}. Arm C decays the state once the update age exceeds a hold time,
\begin{equation}
\hat{v}_d \leftarrow \hat{v}_d\, e^{-\Delta t/\tau_v}\quad \text{for}\quad t - t_{\mathrm{upd}} > t_{\mathrm{hold}},
\end{equation}
with $t_{\mathrm{hold}} = \tau_v = 1$~s. Replaying the production filter offline on the recorded measurement streams reproduces the recorded estimate to 0.3 to 0.8~cm RMS while measurements arrive; with the decay, the largest excursion at 8~s falls from 112 to 33~cm and the tracking segments are untouched. The remaining excursion is the dock continuing to sway while the estimate is frozen, the floor for any estimator without measurements.

\subsection{Relative-frame estimator}
\label{sec:navobs}

A relative-frame estimator was evaluated beside the world-frame filter by offline replay. Its translational state is $x = [r,\; v_d]^\top$ with $r = p_d - p_v$ the dock position relative to the vehicle in world-aligned axes, propagated with the navigation velocity as a known input,
\begin{equation}
r_{k+1} = r_k + (v_{d,k} - \hat{v}_{v,k})\,\Delta t + w_r,\qquad v_{d,k+1} = v_{d,k} + w_v,
\label{eq:rel}
\end{equation}
and updated with the camera-relative translation rotated by attitude only, $z_k = r_k + \nu_k$; the vehicle's world position is never added \cite{kim2007relative}. The uncertainty of the velocity input enters the process noise through $B = [-\Delta t\,I;\; 0]$. Replayed on the recorded trials, it matches the world-frame filter to within 0.5~cm/s of velocity error and 0.8~cm of relative position error when navigation is exact, slightly noisier, and under injected navigation error both filters absorb a slow velocity bias one for one. The limit is structural. The camera measures $r = p_d - p_v$ and the only other input is the navigation solution, so a slow error $b_v$ in $\hat{v}_v$ enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:rel} exactly as dock velocity does, the filter estimates $v_d + b_v$ in either frame, and only the bias's own decay, with $\tau_b = 30$~s over a 60 to 120~s trial, could separate them. What the relative formulation removes is the jitter the world-frame filter makes by differentiating white position noise in the injected navigation error, 1.4 against 4.3~cm/s of fast velocity error; a navigation solution whose position error is the integral of its velocity error has no such white term, so this advantage depends on the error model. The relative-frame estimator was replayed offline only; every live arm, including C, ran the world-frame filter.

Fusion, gating, the velocity bound, both controllers' gains and tolerances, and the state machine are unchanged across all four arms, with the change of units in arm C's position feedback noted above.
