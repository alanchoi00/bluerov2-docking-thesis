\section{Failure Mechanism}
\label{sec:failure}

\subsection{Docking envelope}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pdock} shows the sweep of Section~\ref{sec:design} for arms A and B, three phases per cell, with contact-censored scoring. The baseline docks in every trial at every period and cleanly down to 8~s; at 6~s two of three trials brush the aperture boundary at 0.03 to 0.06~m/s before latching. The feedforward method matches it down to 12~s, degrades at 10~s with one pre-latch contact, and fails at 8 and 6~s, the band of the mean New South Wales wave period. Clean insertions cross the entry plane 0.003 to 0.05~m off axis at 0.06 to 0.11~m/s. The result reproduced on a second host at a different real-time factor and with an earlier filter revision (velocity state unbounded): the baseline docked in every cell, the feedforward method docked in two of three trials at 8~s and one of three at 6~s, and its vehicle-to-dock amplitude ratio rose from 0.97 at 20~s to 1.41 at 8~s and 1.55 at 6~s against 0.85 to 0.98 for the baseline. Retuning the acceleration-noise density over 0.02 to 0.16~m/s$^2$ and bounding the velocity state and covariance limited the size of the estimate excursions but did not move the cliff; at every density the velocity state tracked the true dock velocity (amplitude ratio 0.93 to 1.01, correlation 0.73 to 0.96) while the vehicle over-swung the dock by 1.6 to 1.9 and the trials at 8 and 6~s failed.

\subsection{What the system's own signals say}

In the failed trials the filter's velocity state and the position it extrapolates from it reach many times the true dock amplitude, up to a metre off in position, while every alignment error the controller computes against that estimate reads within seat tolerance. Taken over whole runs, the estimate's excess motion correlates at 0.88 with the vehicle's own position. The natural reading, and the one we first made, is that the vehicle's motion leaks into the world-frame dock measurement through the vehicle pose it is reconstructed with, that the velocity state integrates the leak into a phantom dock velocity, and that the feedforward closes an unstable loop by injecting it into the command. Fig.~\ref{fig:trajectory} shows the failed 8~s trial as that reading sees it: tracking, then a metre-scale excursion of the estimate, then a snap back and a repeat.

\subsection{What the recorded trials say}

Every trial records the fused camera-frame measurement, the transform tree, the filter output, the health signal, the commands and the ground-truth poses of vehicle and dock. This allows the measurement the filter ingested to be rebuilt exactly as the node built it, the transform looked up at the image stamp and applied to the camera-frame pose, and compared with the true dock position. The comparison rejects the reading above on every count (Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}).

\emph{The measurement is clean.} Over all 36 moving-dock trials of both arms the world-frame measurement error is 0.2 to 0.9~cm RMS, with correlations within $\pm 0.07$ against vehicle position, vehicle velocity, range and the number of fused markers. Splitting the error into the part carried by the camera-frame measurement and the part added by the transform path puts the latter at 0.0~cm. Grouping by the set of markers fused shows no subset bias above 0.1~cm, and scanning a time offset on the vehicle pose minimises the error at zero shift. There is no ego-motion leakage to integrate.

\emph{The estimator is right whenever it has measurements.} While markers are visible, the constant-velocity filter tracks the dock to 0.2 to 1.0~cm and its velocity state matches the true dock velocity at every period, including 8 and 6~s, with an amplitude ratio of 0.99 to 1.17 and a correlation of 0.88 to 0.97. The baseline's constant-position filter, by contrast, lags the moving dock by 2 to 7~cm, growing as the period shortens, and docks anyway.

\emph{The feedforward over-drives the vehicle.} What separates the arms is the vehicle's response. In arm B the lateral command in the tracking windows is 90 to 100 percent feedforward, and the vehicle's lateral amplitude over the dock's rises from 1.06 at 20~s to 1.42 at 10~s and 1.7 at 8 and 6~s, the vehicle leading the dock by 10 to 20$^\circ$; in arm A it stays between 0.87 and 1.09 at every period. Fitting the vehicle-velocity response to the command at the dock frequency over every tracking window of both sweeps gives a plant gain of 3.9~m/s per unit command in depth-hold mode and 5.5 in stabilise mode (2.2 and 3.0 by the broadband ratio) with a lag of 0.5 to 0.6~s, against the 1.3 to 1.6 and 0.24~s the feedforward gain had been calibrated to. A feedforward of 0.6 times the dock velocity through such a plant produces 1.3 to 3 times the dock velocity at the vehicle, and the position feedback, acting on an error the lag delays by about half a second, does not pull it back. These closed-loop fits are replaced by the step-test identification of Section~\ref{sec:redesign}; either estimate is well above the assumed value.

\emph{The over-swing is what fails, in two ways.} At 6~s the relative error the over-swing leaves, about 7~cm, sits above the 3~cm alignment tolerance, so the vehicle never advances and times out, with no blackout at all and the estimate within 1~cm throughout. At 8~s the over-swing at 0.2~m range carries the small eye-level markers out of the field of view. The filter reports \textsc{stale} within three seconds, the controller commands zero, the state machine demotes to \textsc{coarse}, and the vehicle drifts. Meanwhile the constant-velocity filter integrates its frozen velocity state, 0.023~m/s in the trial of Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}, for 49~s to a metre off. That excursion is the phantom velocity of the previous subsection. It does not drive the vehicle, because the controller ignores a stale estimate, but it prevents re-acquisition until the dock swings back into view, it snaps the estimate on the next measurement, and it dominates every whole-run statistic: the 0.88 correlation is between a linear ramp of the estimate and a slow drift of the vehicle, with no causal content.

\subsection{Loop interpretation}

The failure is therefore a feedforward loop, not an estimator loop. With feedforward gain $K_{ff}$, plant $G_p(s) = g\,e^{-s\tau}/(1 + sT)$ and a position feedback $C(s)$ on the relative error, the vehicle's response to a dock motion $x_d$ at the sway frequency $\omega$ is
\begin{equation}
\frac{x_v}{x_d} = \frac{K_{ff}\,s\,G_p(s) + C(s)\,G_p(s)}{s + C(s)\,G_p(s)}\Big|_{s = j\omega};
\end{equation}
with $K_{ff}\,g$ between 1.3 and 3 the feedforward term alone exceeds unity, and the feedback term, whose phase the lag $\tau$ and time constant $T$ erode at 8~s, cannot cancel the excess. The amplitude ratio grows as $\omega$ rises because the feedback's authority falls with frequency while the feedforward's does not, which is the trend in the data. The estimator enters only through $\hat{v}_d$, which is accurate; it is the plant model the feedforward was designed against that was wrong, and no estimator setting could repair that, which is why the process-noise sweep found nothing.

The estimator does carry one hazard of its own: a velocity state that keeps integrating while no measurement arrives. Replaying the production filter offline on the recorded measurement streams reproduces the recorded estimate to 0.3 to 0.8~cm RMS while measurements arrive and reproduces the blackout excursions; decaying the velocity state after one second without an accepted update, with a one-second time constant, cuts the 8~s excursion from 112 to 33~cm, the remainder being the dock continuing to sway while the estimate is frozen. That is the estimator half of the redesign.
