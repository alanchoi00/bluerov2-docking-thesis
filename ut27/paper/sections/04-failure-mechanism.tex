\section{Failure Mechanism}
\label{sec:failure}

\subsection{Docking envelope}

The sweep that exposed the failure ran arms A and B at three phases per cell with contact-censored scoring on the first host; Section~\ref{sec:results} repeats all five arms on one host. The baseline docks in every trial at every period and cleanly down to 8~s; at 6~s two of three trials brush the aperture boundary at 0.03 to 0.06~m/s before latching. The feedforward method matches it down to 12~s, degrades at 10~s with one pre-latch contact, and fails at 8 and 6~s (the first host recorded one trial at 8~s and three at 6~s), the band of the mean New South Wales wave period. The result replicated on a second host with an earlier filter revision: baseline 18 of 18, feedforward method 2 of 3 at 8~s and 1 of 3 at 6~s. Retuning the acceleration-noise density over 0.02 to 0.16~m/s$^2$ and bounding the velocity state limited the estimate excursions but did not move the cliff: at every density the velocity state tracked the true dock velocity (amplitude ratio 0.93 to 1.01) while the vehicle over-swung it by 1.6 to 1.9.

\subsection{What the system's own signals say}

In the failed trials the filter's velocity state and the position extrapolated from it reach many times the true dock amplitude, up to a metre off, and correlate at 0.88 with the vehicle's own position. The natural reading, and the one we first made, is that the vehicle's motion leaks into the world-frame measurement and the feedforward closes an unstable loop on the phantom velocity.

\subsection{What the recorded trials say}

Every trial records the fused camera-frame measurement, the transform tree, the filter output, the health signal, the commands and the ground-truth poses, so the measurement the filter ingested can be rebuilt exactly as the node built it and compared with the true dock position. The comparison rejects the reading above on every count (Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}).

\emph{The measurement is clean.} Over the 36 trials of the first-host sweep the world-frame measurement error is 0.2 to 0.9~cm RMS, with correlations within $\pm 0.07$ against vehicle position, vehicle velocity, range and the number of fused markers. Splitting the error into the part carried by the camera-frame measurement and the part added by the transform path puts the latter at 0.0~cm. No marker subset biases the measurement above 0.1~cm, and a time-offset scan on the vehicle pose peaks at zero shift.

\emph{The estimator is right whenever it has measurements.} While markers are visible, the constant-velocity filter tracks the dock to 0.2 to 1.0~cm and its velocity state matches the true dock velocity at every period, including 8 and 6~s, with an amplitude ratio of 0.99 to 1.17 and a correlation of 0.88 to 0.97. The baseline's constant-position filter, by contrast, lags the moving dock by 2 to 7~cm, growing as the period shortens, and docks anyway.

\emph{The feedforward over-drives the vehicle.} What separates the arms is the vehicle's response. In arm B the feedforward dominates the lateral command in the tracking windows, and the vehicle's lateral amplitude over the dock's rises from 1.06 at 20~s to 1.42 at 10~s and 1.7 at 8 and 6~s, the vehicle leading the dock by 10 to 20$^\circ$; in arm A it stays between 0.87 and 1.09 at every period. Open-loop tests with the dock static and the controllers off, steps of $\pm 0.1$ unit held for 8~s from rest and 8 and 6~s sinusoids on each axis in each flight mode, fitted output-error against the ground-truth velocity to a first-order lag with pure delay, identify the command channel as a first-order lag with a steady gain of 2.8~m/s per unit command in depth-hold mode and 2.8 (lateral) and 2.6 (vertical) in stabilise mode, a time constant of 0.25 to 0.40~s and no measurable transport delay, against the 1.3 to 1.6 and 0.24~s the feedforward had been calibrated to. The loop model and the loop design below use one value for every axis and mode, $g = 2.7$~m/s per unit, within 4~percent of each identified gain, and $T = 0.4$~s; the model is insensitive to $T$ over the identified range (1.43 against 1.47 at 8~s). The feedforward alone therefore drives the vehicle at $0.6 \times 2.7 \approx 1.6$ times the dock velocity in the coarse phase and, at unit gain, at about 2.7 times it in the fine phase, where both failures occur; the position feedback, acting on an error the 0.4~s response delays, pulls the fine-phase response back only to the measured 1.6 to 1.7. Regressing the recorded lateral command on the estimated dock velocity and the relative error over the fine-phase windows recovers a feedforward coefficient of 0.64 to 1.13 with the feedback opposing it, so the plant gain sets the over-swing.

\emph{The over-swing causes the failure in two ways.} At 6~s the relative error the over-swing leaves, about 7~cm, sits above the 3~cm alignment tolerance, so the vehicle never advances and times out, with no blackout at all and the estimate within 1~cm throughout. At 8~s the over-swing at 0.2~m range carries the small eye-level markers out of the field of view. The filter reports \textsc{stale} within three seconds, the controller commands zero, the state machine demotes to \textsc{coarse}, and the vehicle drifts. Meanwhile the constant-velocity filter integrates its frozen velocity state, 0.023~m/s in the trial of Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}, for 49~s to a metre off. That excursion is the phantom velocity of the previous subsection. It does not drive the vehicle, because the controller ignores a stale estimate, but it prevents re-acquisition until the dock swings back into view, it snaps the estimate on the next measurement, and it dominates every whole-run statistic: the 0.88 correlation is between a linear ramp of the estimate and a slow drift of the vehicle, with no causal content.

\subsection{Loop interpretation}

The failure is therefore a feedforward loop, not an estimator loop. With feedforward gain $K_{ff}$, plant $G_p(s) = g/(1 + sT)$, with no transport delay measurable in the step tests, and a position feedback $C(s)$ on the relative error, the vehicle's response to a dock motion $x_d$ at the sway frequency $\omega$ is
\begin{equation}
\frac{x_v}{x_d} = \frac{K_{ff}\,s\,G_p(s) + C(s)\,G_p(s)}{s + C(s)\,G_p(s)}\Big|_{s = j\omega},
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}
with $C(s) = K_p + K_d s$, $K_p = 0.8$ and $K_d = 0.3$ effort per metre and per m/s in the fine phase. With $K_{ff}\,g$ of 1.6 in the coarse phase and 2.7 in the fine phase the feedforward term alone exceeds unity, and the feedback attenuates the excess by a return ratio that falls from about 7 at 20~s to 2.7 at 8~s because of the integrator between velocity and position, so the ratio grows with $\omega$, which is the trend in the data (Section~\ref{sec:results}). The estimator enters through $\hat{v}_d$, whose amplitude is accurate, so no estimator setting could remove the over-swing; its phase lag still matters at 8~s, where Section~\ref{sec:results} shows the oracle seating and the original method not.

The estimator does carry one hazard of its own, a velocity state that keeps integrating while no measurement arrives; Section~\ref{sec:redesign} bounds it.
