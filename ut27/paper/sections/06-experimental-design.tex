\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

Four arms share the perception pipeline, the controllers and the state machine and differ in the estimator regime and the feedforward law (Table~\ref{tab:arms}): (A) the reactive baseline, static regime, no feedforward; (B) the original method, sway regime with the open-loop feedforward of Section~\ref{sec:system}; (C) the velocity-closed feedforward of Section~\ref{sec:redesign}; and (D) an oracle that feeds forward the ground-truth dock velocity through the same law, used only as a diagnostic bound. If D over-swings as B does, the plant is the limit; if C approaches D, the mechanism and the fix are confirmed.

The core sweep covers periods of 20, 16, 12, 10, 8 and 6~s at 0.1~m orbital amplitude, three start phases per cell. Every trial starts from the same spawn 5~m from the dock, engages the state machine once the filter has initialised, and runs to the seat or a 200~s timeout; a run stopped by the wall-clock guard when the simulator fell below a real-time factor of 0.25 was rerun. A trial receives one of three outcomes: \emph{clean} if the vehicle latched without its body origin crossing the 0.15~m entry aperture off axis before latching; \emph{contact} if it crossed the aperture boundary inside the dock plane before latching, counted as docked if it then latched but not as clean, since contact is not physically modelled; and \emph{no dock} on timeout. Three trials per cell cannot distinguish 3 of 3 from 0 of 3 (two-sided Fisher exact test, $p = 0.10$), so per-cell fractions are descriptive and the comparisons are made on counts pooled over cells or levels, with exact tests and 95~percent Wilson intervals. Per trial the analysis records the lateral and vertical offset and the closing speed at the dock plane, the time to dock, the vehicle-to-dock lateral amplitude ratio over the measured tracking windows, the dock-velocity estimation error against the ground-truth derivative while the filter is usable, the fraction of time without a marker measurement, and the counts of demotions and stale episodes. All of these are computed offline from recorded bags by the same scripts for every arm.

The navigation-robustness sweep injects a first-order Gauss-Markov velocity bias into the vehicle navigation solution seen by the estimators and controllers, with the ground truth used only for scoring:
\begin{equation}
b^v_{k+1} = \rho\, b^v_k + w_k,\qquad \rho = e^{-\Delta t/\tau_b},
\end{equation}
with $w_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_b^2 (1-\rho^2))$ so that $\sigma_b$ is the stationary standard deviation of the bias,
integrated into a position error $b^p_{k+1} = b^p_k + \Delta t\, b^v_k$, plus white velocity noise of 5~mm/s and white position noise of 5~mm in the offline replay and 1~mm in the live sweep: 5~mm of white noise on the relayed vehicle pose kept the coarse handoff's differenced-velocity gate from latching in every arm, so the live level was lowered to one at which the gate behaves as it does with exact navigation. The correlation time is 30~s and three levels of $\sigma_b$, 0.01, 0.03 and 0.10~m/s, are swept at the 10 and 8~s periods for arms A to C, with one seed, so the three trials of a cell share the bias realisation and differ in sway start phase. No navigation error statistics were available for this vehicle, so this is a sensitivity sweep, not a model of a particular sensor \cite{brown2012random,groves2013principles}; the levels bracket a Doppler-aided solution. The envelope of Section~\ref{sec:failure} was recorded on a first host and replicated on a second; the comparison of Section~\ref{sec:results} reruns all four arms on a third host and one code revision, with the filter stepping on simulation time so that the outcome does not depend on the real-time factor. The navigation-error sweep is evaluated first by offline replay of both estimators on the recorded measurement streams of four arm-B trials, which isolates the estimators from the controllers; the live sweep additionally exposes the controllers and the handoff gate to the corrupted navigation.
