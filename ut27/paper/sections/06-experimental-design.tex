\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

Four arms share the perception pipeline, the controllers and the state machine and differ only in the estimator and the feedforward source (Table~\ref{tab:design}): (A) the reactive baseline, static regime, no feedforward; (B) the original method, sway regime with the open-loop feedforward of Section~\ref{sec:system}; (C) the redesign of Section~\ref{sec:redesign}; and (D) an oracle that feeds forward the ground-truth dock velocity through the same law, used only as a diagnostic bound. If D over-swings as B does, the plant is the limit; if C approaches D, the mechanism and the fix are confirmed.

The core sweep covers periods of 20, 16, 12, 10, 8 and 6~s at 0.1~m orbital amplitude, three start phases per cell at the long periods and eight at 12~s and below, with one static-dock cell per arm. Every trial starts from the same spawn 5~m from the dock, engages the state machine once the filter has initialised, and runs to the seat or a timeout. A trial receives one of three outcomes: \emph{clean} if the vehicle latched without crossing the 0.15~m entry aperture off axis before latching; \emph{contact} if it crossed the aperture boundary inside the dock plane before latching, since contact is not physically modelled the trial is censored from that instant; and \emph{no dock} on timeout. Per trial the analysis records the lateral and vertical offset and the closing speed at the dock plane, the time to dock, the vehicle-to-dock lateral amplitude ratio over the measured tracking windows, the dock-velocity estimation error against the ground-truth derivative while the filter is usable, the fraction of time without a marker measurement, and the counts of demotions and stale episodes. All of these are computed offline from recorded bags by the same scripts for every arm.

The navigation-robustness sweep injects a first-order Gauss-Markov velocity bias into the vehicle navigation solution seen by the estimators and controllers, with the ground truth used only for scoring:
\begin{equation}
b^v_{k+1} = \rho\, b^v_k + w_k,\qquad \rho = e^{-\Delta t/\tau_b},
\end{equation}
with $w_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_b^2 (1-\rho^2))$ so that $\sigma_b$ is the stationary standard deviation of the bias,
integrated into a position error $b^p_{k+1} = b^p_k + \Delta t\, b^v_k$, plus white position and velocity noise of 5~mm and 5~mm/s. The correlation time is 30~s and three levels of $\sigma_b$, 0.01, 0.03 and 0.10~m/s, are swept at the 10 and 8~s periods for arms A to C. No empirical statistics for the vehicle's navigation exist, so this is presented as a sensitivity sweep, not as a model of a particular sensor \cite{brown2012random,groves2013principles,paull2014auv}. The sweep of Section~\ref{sec:failure} was additionally replicated on a second host at a different real-time factor and with an earlier filter revision, to separate the estimator design from compute load and incidental tuning.
